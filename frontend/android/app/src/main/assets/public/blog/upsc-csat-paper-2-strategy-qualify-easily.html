UPSC CSAT Paper 2 Strategy: How to Qualify Easily

Complete UPSC CSAT (Paper II) preparation strategy. Learn how to score 66+ marks in comprehension, logical reasoning, math, and decision making.

G
By GovtExamPath Team
WhatsAppTelegramTwitter

What Is CSAT and Why Many Aspirants Fear It

CSAT (Civil Services Aptitude Test) is Paper II of the UPSC Prelims examination. It was introduced in 2011 to test the aptitude and analytical abilities of candidates. Since 2015, it is qualifying in nature — you need to score just 33% (66 out of 200 marks) to qualify.

Despite the low qualifying mark, every year thousands of aspirants fail to clear CSAT. In 2026, an estimated 15-20% of candidates who cleared GS Paper I cutoff were eliminated because they couldn't score 66 in CSAT. Don't let this be you.

CSAT Paper Pattern

| Topic | Approximate Questions | Marks | |-------|----------------------|-------| | Comprehension | 25-30 | 50-60 | | Logical Reasoning & Analytical Ability | 15-20 | 30-40 | | Basic Numeracy (Class 10 level math) | 15-18 | 30-36 | | Data Interpretation | 5-8 | 10-16 | | Decision Making & Problem Solving | 5-8 | 10-16 | | English Comprehension (for non-English medium) | 5-8 | 10-16 | | Total | 80 | 200 |

Time allotted: 2 hours (120 minutes). Negative marking: 1/3rd of marks for wrong answers.

Topic-Wise Strategy

1. Comprehension (25-30 questions) — Your Lifeline

This is where you score maximum marks with minimum preparation:

Types of passages:

  • Factual passages (straightforward — read and answer)
  • Inferential passages (you need to derive conclusions)
  • Abstract/philosophical passages (the trickiest — focus on the author's central argument)

How to approach:

  1. Read the questions FIRST, then the passage
  2. Underline key phrases that match question keywords
  3. Eliminate obviously wrong options
  4. For inference questions, choose the option that is MOST supported by the passage — not your personal opinion
  5. Don't spend more than 2 minutes per comprehension question

Tip: If English is your strong suit, comprehension alone can give you 50-60 marks — enough to qualify.

2. Basic Numeracy (15-18 questions) — Class 10 Level

Topics frequently tested:

  • Percentages, Ratios, Averages — Most common (5-6 questions)
  • Profit & Loss, Simple/Compound Interest — 2-3 questions
  • Time, Speed, Distance and Work — 2-3 questions
  • Number series and patterns — 2-3 questions
  • Basic algebra and equations — 1-2 questions

Key approach:

  • These are NOT SSC-level math questions. They are simpler but require logical thinking
  • Focus on understanding the concept, not memorizing formulas
  • Learn approximation techniques — exact calculations are rarely needed
  • Practice with UPSC previous year CSAT papers, not random math books

3. Logical Reasoning (15-20 questions)

Common types:

  • Syllogisms: All A are B, Some B are C, therefore...
  • Blood Relations: A is the father of B's mother. How is A related to B?
  • Direction/Distance problems: Walk North 5km, turn left, walk 3km...
  • Coding-Decoding: If APPLE = ELPPA, then MANGO = ?
  • Arrangements: Seating, ranking, scheduling problems

Tip: Practice 5 reasoning questions daily for 2 months. Focus on the Venn diagram method for syllogisms — it's foolproof.

4. Data Interpretation (5-8 questions)

  • Bar graphs, pie charts, line graphs, tables
  • Usually involves calculating percentages, ratios, or finding maximum/minimum values
  • Tip: Learn to read data quickly. Practice approximation — CSAT DI doesn't need exact calculations

5. Decision Making (5-8 questions)

These present a scenario and ask what action should be taken. Common in government/administrative contexts:

  • Read ALL options before answering
  • Choose the most balanced, reasonable option
  • Avoid extreme choices (always/never/immediately punish)
  • Think from a government administrator's perspective
  • Ethics and fairness are usually the correct guiding principles

The 30-Day CSAT Preparation Plan

Week 1: Comprehension Mastery

  • Day 1-3: Practice 5 comprehension passages daily from previous UPSC papers
  • Day 4-5: Work on inference-based questions specifically
  • Day 6-7: Timed practice — 5 passages in 30 minutes

Week 2: Mathematics Revival

  • Day 8-10: Revise basic arithmetic concepts (percentages, ratios, averages)
  • Day 11-12: Time-speed-distance and work problems
  • Day 13-14: Number series, basic algebra

Week 3: Reasoning & DI

  • Day 15-17: Logical reasoning — syllogisms, blood relations, direction
  • Day 18-19: Seating arrangements and ranking
  • Day 20-21: Data Interpretation — charts and tables

Week 4: Mock Tests & Revision

  • Day 22-24: Full CSAT mock tests (take 3 complete papers)
  • Day 25-26: Analyze mocks, revise weak areas
  • Day 27-28: One more mock + decision making practice
  • Day 29-30: Light revision, formula sheet review

Scoring Strategy for the Exam Day

Target: 80-100 marks (way above the 66 qualifying mark)

| Priority | Section | Time | Target | |----------|---------|------|--------| | 1st | Comprehension | 50 min | 45-55 marks | | 2nd | Reasoning | 30 min | 20-25 marks | | 3rd | Mathematics | 25 min | 15-20 marks | | 4th | DI + Decision Making | 15 min | 10-15 marks |

Important exam-day tips:

  • Attempt comprehension FIRST — it's the most scoring with least risk
  • Skip difficult math questions — they carry the same marks as easy ones
  • For reasoning, draw diagrams on the rough sheet
  • Leave 10 minutes for revision and OMR sheet filling
  • Don't guess randomly — negative marking can pull you below 66

Resources for CSAT Preparation

  1. Previous Year Papers (2011-2026) — The single best resource. CSAT patterns repeat
  2. NCERT Mathematics (Class 9-10) — For basic numeracy revision
  3. Tata McGraw Hill CSAT Manual — Comprehensive practice book
  4. RS Aggarwal Verbal & Non-Verbal Reasoning — For reasoning practice

The Bottom Line

CSAT is a qualifying paper — treat it that way. Don't spend months preparing for it at the cost of GS Paper I. A focused 30-day preparation is enough for most candidates. If math is your weakness, compensate with comprehension. The 66-mark target is very achievable with basic preparation.
