Banking Exam Preparation: Complete Guide for IBPS PO & SBI PO 2026

Master banking exam preparation with tips for IBPS PO and SBI PO 2026. Covers Prelims, Mains strategy, section-wise approach, and interview preparation.

G
By GovtExamPath Team
WhatsAppTelegramTwitter

Why Banking Exams Are a Great Career Choice

Banking sector jobs are among the most stable and well-paying government positions in India. With IBPS and SBI conducting annual recruitment drives for 10,000+ positions, banking exams offer excellent opportunities for graduates from any discipline.

Key Benefits:

  • Starting salary of Rs 52,000-65,000/month (including DA and allowances)
  • Regular promotions — PO to Manager in 3 years, Senior Manager in 7 years
  • Perks: Housing allowance, medical insurance, pension, leave encashment
  • Work-life balance with defined working hours
  • Transfer to preferred locations after initial posting

IBPS PO vs SBI PO: Key Differences

| Aspect | IBPS PO | SBI PO | |--------|---------|--------| | Banks | 11 Public Sector Banks | State Bank of India only | | Vacancies | 4,000-6,000 | 2,000-3,000 | | Difficulty | Moderate | Slightly harder | | Prelims Pattern | Same | Same | | Mains | 200 marks + Descriptive | 200 marks + Descriptive | | Salary | Rs 52,000+ | Rs 56,000+ |

Prelims Strategy (Common for Both)

The Prelims exam is a qualifying round. You need to clear sectional cutoffs and overall cutoff.

Exam Pattern: | Section | Questions | Marks | Time | |---------|-----------|-------|------| | English Language | 30 | 30 | 20 min | | Quantitative Aptitude | 35 | 35 | 20 min | | Reasoning Ability | 35 | 35 | 20 min |

English Language (Target: 20-24 marks)

High-scoring topics to focus on:

  • Reading Comprehension (10-12 questions) — Read the passage carefully, eliminate wrong options
  • Cloze Test (5-6 questions) — Focus on grammar and context
  • Error Detection (5 questions) — Learn common error patterns
  • Fill in the Blanks (5 questions) — Vocabulary based

Preparation tip: Read banking/finance articles from Livemint or Business Standard daily. This improves both comprehension speed and banking awareness simultaneously.

Quantitative Aptitude (Target: 22-28 marks)

This section requires speed and accuracy:

  • Simplification/Approximation (5 questions) — Never miss these, they're the easiest
  • Data Interpretation (10-15 questions) — Practice different types: bar, pie, line, table, caselet
  • Number Series (5 questions) — Learn common patterns
  • Arithmetic Word Problems (10 questions) — Percentage, Profit/Loss, CI/SI, Time & Work

Preparation tip: Learn calculation shortcuts. Memorize squares up to 30, cubes up to 15, and common fractions-to-percentage conversions.

Reasoning Ability (Target: 24-30 marks)

The most predictable section:

  • Puzzles & Seating Arrangement (15-20 questions) — The backbone of reasoning. Practice 5 puzzles daily
  • Syllogism (3-5 questions) — Learn Venn diagram method, never miss these
  • Inequality (5 questions) — Easy marks with practice
  • Coding-Decoding, Blood Relations, Direction (5-10 questions)

Mains Strategy

Key Sections in Mains

Reasoning & Computer Aptitude (60 questions, 60 marks, 45 min)

  • Puzzles become complex — 4-5 variable puzzles, floor-based arrangements
  • Computer aptitude: Basic hardware, software, networking, MS Office
  • Data Sufficiency questions

Data Analysis & Interpretation (35 questions, 60 marks, 45 min)

  • Advanced DI: Missing DI, caselet DI, combinational charts
  • Quantity comparison questions
  • Probability and permutation/combination

General/Banking/Economy Awareness (40 questions, 40 marks, 35 min)

  • Banking awareness: RBI policies, types of accounts, banking terms, BASEL norms
  • Current affairs: Last 3-4 months — focus on banking, economy, appointments, awards
  • Static GK: Headquarters of banks, financial organizations, currency & capital

English Language (35 questions, 40 marks, 40 min)

  • RC passages are longer and complex
  • Vocabulary-based questions increase
  • Connectors, paragraph completion, sentence rearrangement

Descriptive Paper (Letter + Essay)

30 minutes, 50 marks. Usually 1 essay (200-250 words) + 1 formal letter.

Common topics:

  • Financial inclusion in India
  • Digital banking and cashless economy
  • Role of RBI in economic stability
  • Government schemes (PM Jan Dhan Yojana, Mudra, etc.)

Tip: Practice writing 1 essay and 1 letter every week. Keep it structured: Introduction, 3 body paragraphs, conclusion.

60-Day Preparation Plan

Days 1-20: Foundation

  • Complete basic concepts in all three Prelims sections
  • Start banking awareness notes (current + static)
  • Daily: 30 min newspaper + 50 quant questions + 3 puzzles

Days 21-40: Intensive Practice

  • Start taking Prelims mock tests (one every 2 days)
  • Begin Mains-level DI and reasoning puzzles
  • Current affairs compilation — last 4 months
  • Daily: English RC practice + 5 DI sets

Days 41-60: Mock Test Phase

  • Daily mock tests for Prelims section
  • Analyze every mock — maintain an error log
  • Weekly Mains mocks for practice
  • Descriptive writing practice — 2 per week
  • Final revision of banking awareness and current affairs

Must-Know Banking Terms

Every banking exam aspirant must know these:

  • Repo Rate — Rate at which RBI lends to banks (currently 5.25%)
  • Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) — Rate at which banks deposit with RBI (replaced Reverse Repo as floor rate, currently 5.00%)
  • CRR — Cash Reserve Ratio banks maintain with RBI
  • SLR — Statutory Liquidity Ratio
  • NPA — Non-Performing Asset (loan not repaid for 90+ days)
  • CASA — Current Account Savings Account ratio
  • KYC — Know Your Customer norms
  • NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, UPI — Different payment systems

Interview Preparation (After Mains)

The interview carries 100 marks (IBPS) or 50 marks (SBI equivalent weight). Key areas:

  1. Introduction — Prepare a crisp 2-minute introduction covering education, hobbies, and why banking
  2. Why banking? — Have a genuine answer linking your background to the banking sector
  3. Current banking news — RBI policies, major bank mergers, digital banking trends
  4. Basic banking concepts — Expect questions on the terms listed above
  5. Your graduation subject — They will ask questions from your degree subject
